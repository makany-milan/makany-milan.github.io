excerpt: 'Science disproportionately relies on the most productive researchers to evaluate the work of others, potentially diverting their scarce time from other tasks. We study this trade-off in Italy’s national academic qualification system, where evaluators are randomly assigned to committees. Committees including better-published researchers make more informative selection decisions, particularly for more junior applicants: their qualified candidates have stronger subsequent citation and career outcomes, while their rejected candidates perform worse. However, these benefits come with substantial opportunity costs. Committee service reduces evaluators’ publication output and PhD supervision, with larger research losses among more productive researchers and those working in smaller teams.'
