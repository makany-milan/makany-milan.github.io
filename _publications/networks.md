excerpt: 'Women tend to have smaller professional networks which may increase gaps in labor-market outcomes. Gender differences in networks can arise for two reasons: (i) unequal opportunities to meet potential connections or (ii) differences in forming new ties conditional on meeting. I distinguish between these margins using the random assignment of researchers to national evaluation committees in Italian academia. Serving together on a committee increases the probability of coauthoring during the subsequent eight years by 40 percent relative to other pairs who could have been assigned to the same committee but were not. Conditional on meeting, women are just as likely to form new collaborations as men, and same-gender pairs are not more prone to collaborate than mixed-gender pairs. These results suggest that observational gender differences in network size and gender-homophily are unlikely to arise due to gender-specific preferences. Instead, unequal access to opportunities for interaction is a more plausible explanation.'
